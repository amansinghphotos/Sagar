# Chete

Chete (ਚੇਤੇ, "remembered") keeps what you copy, so that you can find it again and paste it into any app.
Its identifier is `com.amansingh.chete`, the same as the Chete that came before it, so the permission
already given to that one carries over.

## What It Is For

You copy as you always do. Later, in any app, you press one key, type a few letters of what you want,
and press Return: Chete pastes it where you were. Chete sends nothing anywhere: your history stays on
this Mac unless you carry it.

## Must-Haves

1. **It keeps what you copy.** Text, links, pictures, and files copied in Finder, newest first.
   For a file it keeps where the file is, not a second copy. Everything is encrypted on this Mac, with
   a key that is made by itself, kept only on this Mac and never asks anything of you.
   Passwords are kept too, even when the app they come from marks them private, so that you can paste
   one again later. What you copy on your own iPhone or another of your Macs, which macOS hands over
   by itself, is kept too, unless you turn that off.
   *Asks for:* Paste from Other Apps, once, in System Settings, to keep what you copy. Until you choose
   Allow there, macOS asks before each copy is kept.
2. **It opens from any app,** with a keyboard shortcut you choose, and from its item in the menu bar.
   You choose the shortcut in the welcome, and can change it in Settings. Chete suggests three. The
   first is the one the earlier Chete used, read from its code, and is already chosen. A keyboard
   lights each key you press. It warns you when macOS or Chete already uses those keys. It asks for
   Command or Control, as macOS may refuse a shortcut without one. It cannot warn of another app's
   shortcut. It lives in the menu bar and has no Dock icon.
3. **It finds as you type.** Each letter narrows the list. The arrow keys move, and Return picks.
4. **It pastes for you** into the app you were in, or pastes as plain text if you hold Shift. If you
   have not allowed it to paste, it copies, and says to press Command-V.
   *Asks for:* Accessibility, once, in System Settings, to paste for you.
5. **It keeps things as long as you choose:** a week, a month, a year or forever, a year at first.
   You can delete one thing, or everything.
6. **Your history goes with you in one file.** Export saves everything in a plain file you choose,
   which opens on any Mac with no key or password, so keep it somewhere safe: it holds every clip,
   passwords included. Bring Back reads such a file on any Mac and replaces what is there.
7. **The first open on a new Mac is a welcome** that a person new to computers can finish alone.
   It greets you in Gurmukhi and in English, and says what Chete does in two lines. Those lines say
   it opens from the menu bar or, quicker, with a shortcut. Then it sets Chete up, one step to a
   page: your shortcut, keeping what you copy, and pasting for you. Last come the offers to bring
   back an exported file and to open at login. For each permission it opens System Settings, and
   steps aside as a small card that stays in front. The card names the place and shows the switch
   to look for. Only you can turn it on. The welcome keeps checking, and goes on by itself once it
   is on. Pages arrive and leave with Look's motion. Any step can be skipped, and waits in Settings.
   It never asks for a Claude key.
   The Gurmukhi shows where Chete's name comes from, in three places, each with its English beside
   it. The greeting is ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ ("welcome"). The name is ਚੇਤੇ. The last line is ਹੁਣ ਸਭ ਕੁਝ ਚੇਤੇ ਰਹੇਗਾ
   ("now everything will be remembered"). Nothing you need is said in Gurmukhi alone.
   *Asks for:* Login Items, if you choose to open it at login. The Gurmukhi is set in a typeface macOS
   carries, so no font is added.
8. **It takes over the Chete before it.** On its first open on a Mac where the earlier Chete kept a
   history, it brings in every clip and both settings, how long to keep things and whether copies from
   other devices arrive. macOS asks you once, and you choose Always Allow. The earlier history is read
   and never changed or deleted.
9. **It keeps itself up to date,** as every app here does: it shows what is new, and you choose when.

## The Network

It works offline. Once a day it fetches its update feed from this site, and it downloads an update
only when you choose to install it.

## Out of Scope

- The same history on every Mac through iCloud. Export and Bring Back carry it instead.
- Anything that talks to Claude.
- Pinned clips, snippets, and editing a clip before pasting it.
